Arabic Reading & Writing at Home — Guide for the Older Sister

A practical plan for a child (roughly 6–9) who already knows the Arabic letters with tashkeel (she can read بَ بِ بُ on their own) but cannot yet read or write joined words. It takes her from single letters to reading short, fully-vowelled stories and writing sentences, in 20–30 minutes a day.

It is written in English for the parent, with every example in fully-vowelled Arabic.

Where she is, and where she's going

She has done the hardest memorisation already: 28 letters and 3 short vowels. What's left is a short list of rules, and each one unlocks a lot of words. Teach them in this order, one per week, and don't skip ahead:

Stage Rule What she can read after it
1 Letters change shape when they join (and 6 letters never join to the left) كَتَبَ، ذَهَبَ، سَمِعَ — any word with only short vowels
2 Long vowels: ا after fatha, و after damma, ي after kasra قَالَ، يَقُولُ، فِي، كَبِير
3 Sukoon: the letter has no vowel, glue it to the letter before مِنْ، يَكْتُبُ، بَيْت، يَوْم
4 Tanween: a doubled vowel mark at the end = add "n" كِتَابٌ، قَلَمًا، بَيْتٍ
5 Shadda: say the letter twice أُمّ، سَيَّارَة، مُعَلِّم
6 ال: moon letters say the ل, sun letters skip it and double the next letter الْقَمَر، الشَّمْس
7 ة at the end, ى at the end, and the five shapes of hamza (ء أ إ ؤ ئ) مَدْرَسَة، عَلَى، سُؤَال
8 Sentences and short stories Everything with tashkeel

The short version

  1. Print the three PDFs. Hang the rules sheet. Keep the reading and writing pages in a folder.
  2. Every day, 25 minutes: 3-minute warm-up (letter forms flash), 10 minutes on this week's reading page, 10 minutes writing (trace → join → copy → 3-word dictation), then you read her an Arabic story for 5 minutes. Weekends off.
  3. One rule a week. Read the rule from 01-arabic-reading-rules.md, say it in one sentence to her, then let the page do the teaching. Repeat a week whenever the page isn't fluent by Friday. Repeating is normal, not failing.
  4. Every reading row three times: you read while she follows with her finger; together; she alone. Then she ticks the box.
  5. Writing is copying, then dictation. Every day she copies 4 words from the reading page and takes a 3-word dictation from yesterday's page.

Golden rules

  • Vowels are not optional. For at least a year she reads only fully-vowelled text (نص مشكول). Unvowelled text comes after fluency.
  • Tap, then slide. Finger taps each letter as she says letter+vowel (بَ – تَ), then slides under the whole word (بَتَ). This is the Arabic equivalent of blending.
  • Short vowel = one clap, long vowel = two claps. Exaggerate the difference. Most reading mistakes at this stage are short/long confusion.
  • Never let her guess from the first letter. Cover the rest of the word with your thumb and reveal one letter at a time.
  • Right to left, always, in reading and writing. Put a small sticker on the right edge of her paper as the "start" mark until it is automatic.
  • Dots after the body. When writing, she finishes the letter or word body, then goes back and adds dots and hamzas, then vowels.
  • Short beats long. Ten focused minutes on one page beats thirty minutes on three. Stop while it's still going well.
  • Read to her in Arabic every day. Five minutes of you reading a vowelled story aloud, finger under the words, is half of the learning.

The Rules of Reading Arabic, in Teaching Order

This is the "basic Arabic stuff" in one place: everything a child needs to go from single letters to reading vowelled text. Each rule is stated the way you'd say it to her, followed by what you need to know as the teacher. The printable printables/rules-sheet.pdf is the one-page version to hang up.

Rule 0 — What she already knows (recap)

The 28 letters, and the three short vowels (الحَرَكَات):

Mark Name Sound Example
ـَ فَتْحَة fatha short "a" as in cat (or "u" in but) بَ
ـِ كَسْرَة kasra short "i" as in sit بِ
ـُ ضَمَّة damma short "u" as in put بُ

Check she can do this fast with any letter, in random order, in one breath: تَ تِ تُ · جُ جَ جِ · عِ عَ عُ. If a letter is slow, that letter goes on the daily warm-up pile. Everything below assumes this is automatic.

Rule 1 — Letters hold hands (joining) — Week 1–2

Say to her: "In a word, letters hold hands. When a letter holds hands it loses its tail, but keeps its body and its dots."

  • Every letter has up to four shapes: alone, at the start, in the middle, at the end. ب · بـ · ـبـ · ـب
  • The body (the part with the dots) stays the same. Only the tail/hook changes. So she is not learning 100 new letters; she is learning to spot the body.
  • Teach shapes in families that share a body: 1. ب ت ث ن ي (the tooth family; note ن and ي change more at the end: ـن ـي) 2. ج ح خ 3. ا د ذ ر ز و (the six that never hold hands to the left, see below) 4. س ش ص ض 5. ط ظ ع غ (ع and غ lose their open "mouth" in the middle: ـعـ ـغـ) 6. ف ق ك ل م (ك becomes ـكـ in the middle; م becomes ـمـ) 7. ه ة ء لا (ه has the strangest middle form ـهـ; لا is one shape for ل + ا)

The six that don't hold hands to the left (حروف لا تتصل بما بعدها): ا د ذ ر ز و. They connect to the letter before them (on the right) but never to the letter after them (on the left). So the word breaks after them and the next letter starts fresh: دَرَسَ = دَ + رَ + سَ (three pieces), وَلَد = وَ + لَد. Teach her the little rhyme: "أ، د، ذ، ر، ز، و — six letters that let go."

How to read a joined word (this is the core skill): 1. Find each letter's body and its vowel. 2. Tap and say each letter with its vowel: كَ – تَ – بَ. 3. Slide the finger under the whole word and say it fast: كَتَبَ.

Start with 3-letter words with only short vowels (كَتَبَ، ذَهَبَ، سَمِعَ، جَلَسَ، لَعِبَ) and 2-letter words (لَكَ، مَعَ، هُوَ، هِيَ). No madd, no sukoon yet: those are Weeks 3 and 5. Four-letter words with only short vowels are rare, so don't hunt for them; 3-letter words are enough to make joining automatic.

Rule 2 — Long vowels: حُرُوف المَدّ — Week 3–4

Say to her: "Three letters can be stretched. ا after a fatha, و after a damma, ي after a kasra. When a letter is stretched, it has no vowel of its own: it just makes the vowel before it long. Clap twice."

Long vowel Written Example Sound
fatha + ا ـَا قَالَ، نَامَ، بَابَ "aa"
damma + و ـُو يَقُولُ، نُور "oo"
kasra + ي ـِي فِي، كَبِير، يَبِيعُ "ee"
  • The madd letter carries no haraka (no mark, or in some books a tiny sukoon-free space). If ي or و carries a vowel, it's a consonant (يَد، وَلَد), not a madd.
  • Short vs long is the big battle. Do minimal pairs out loud: جَمَل / جَمَال، عَلَم / عَالَم، سَلَم / سَلاَم. One clap vs two claps.
  • Combination: مَدّ with an alif hamza on top is written آ (= أَا): آدَم، قُرْآن.

Rule 3 — Sukoon: السُّكُون — Week 5

Say to her: "A little circle on a letter means: no vowel. Don't add a sound. Just glue that letter to the one before it."

  • ـْ on a letter: say the letter's sound with no vowel, joined to the previous syllable: مِنْ = مِ + نْ = "min". يَكْتُبُ = يَكْ – تُ – بُ.
  • Reading trick: the letter before the sukoon carries it. Tap them as a pair: مَنْ (one tap), يَلْ – عَ – بُ.
  • Two special sukoon pairs (the diphthongs):
  • fatha + يْ = "ay": بَيْت، عَيْن، كَيْفَ، لَيْل
  • fatha + وْ = "aw": يَوْم، لَوْن، نَوْم، خَوْف
  • The first letter of a word never has sukoon. The last letter of a sentence is usually read with sukoon when you stop (وَقْف), even if it has a vowel: قَلَمٌ at the end is read "qalam". Teach this in Week 10 with sentences, not before.

Rule 4 — Tanween: التَّنْوِين — Week 6

Say to her: "Two of the same vowel mark at the end of a word means: add an 'n' sound. Only at the end."

Tanween Written Sound Example
tanween fath ـً (with an extra alif) "an" كِتَابًا، قَلَمًا، بَيْتًا
tanween damm ـٌ "un" كِتَابٌ، قَلَمٌ، بَيْتٌ
tanween kasr ـٍ "in" كِتَابٍ، قَلَمٍ، بَيْتٍ
  • Tanween fath is written with an alif that is silent (كِتَابًا: the alif is not a madd, it's just the "seat" of the tanween). Exceptions: no alif after ة (مَدْرَسَةً) or after hamza-on-alif (خَطَأً) or alif (سَمَاءً is written on the hamza).
  • The "n" is heard but no ن is written. This is why dictation gets tricky: "qalamun" has no ن.
  • Practise nouns in all three: بَابٌ · بَابًا · بَابٍ.

Rule 5 — Shadda: الشَّدَّة — Week 7

Say to her: "The little 'w' on top means: say the letter twice. First time with sukoon, second time with its vowel."

  • ـّ : the letter is doubled. رَبّ = رَبْ + بَ. مُعَلِّم = مُ – عَلْ – لِ – م.
  • The shadda's vowel sits above it for fatha and damma (بَّ، بُّ) and, in most children's books, below the shadda but above the letter for kasra (بِّ). Show her both.
  • Shadda + tanween is common at word ends: أُمٌّ، جَدٌّ، حَجٌّ، قِطٌّ، دُبٌّ.
  • Practice with rhythm: clap–clap on the doubled letter: سَيْـ يَا رَة.

Rule 6 — The definite article ال — Week 8

Say to her: "ال at the start means 'the'. With half the letters you say the ل, with the other half the ل disappears and the next letter gets a shadda."

  • Moon letters (الحروف القَمَرِيَّة) — the ل is pronounced, and it carries a sukoon: ا ب ج ح خ ع غ ف ق ك م ه و ي الْقَمَر، الْبَاب، الْكِتَاب، الْوَلَد، الْبَيْت، الْعَيْن، الْمَاء، الْهَوَاء Memory sentence: إِبْغِ حَجَّكَ وَخَفْ عَقِيمَهُ (the classic one; each letter in it is a moon letter).
  • Sun letters (الحروف الشَّمْسِيَّة) — the ل is written but silent, and the next letter takes a shadda: ت ث د ذ ر ز س ش ص ض ط ظ ل ن الشَّمْس، النَّهْر، الدَّرْس، السَّمَاء، الرَّجُل، الطَّرِيق، الصَّفّ، اللَّيْل، التُّفَّاح، الزَّهْرَة Reading cue: if the ل has no sukoon and the next letter has a shadda, it's a sun letter: skip the ل.
  • Rule of thumb for her: "Look at the ل. Sukoon on it → say it. Nothing on it and a shadda next door → don't say it."
  • The alif of ال is a hamzat wasl: it's pronounced "a" at the start of speech (الْبَيْت = "al-bayt") but silent when the word before runs into it (فِي الْبَيْت = "fil-bayt"). Introduce this only in Week 10 with sentences.

Rule 7 — Endings and hamza shapes — Week 9

ة (تاء مربوطة)

  • Only at the end of a word. It says "t" when you keep going, "h" when you stop. مَدْرَسَةٌ (madrasatun) → stopping: مَدْرَسَة (madrasah).
  • Most feminine words end with it: مُعَلِّمَة، سَيَّارَة، شَجَرَة، فَاطِمَة.
  • Writing rule: it's a ه with two dots. Don't confuse with ه (his/him ending: كِتَابُهُ) which has no dots.

ى (أَلِف مَقْصُورَة)

  • Only at the end. Looks like ي without dots. Sounds like a long "aa": عَلَى، إِلَى، مَتَى، مُوسَى، عِيسَى، مُسْتَشْفَى.
  • Compare: ي with dots at the end is "ee" (فِي، عَلِي) or "y".

The five shapes of hamza (ء)

All are the same sound: the catch in the throat at the start of "apple" said sharply.

Shape Where Examples
أ (on alif, above) start of word with fatha/damma; sometimes middle/end أَحْمَد، أُمّ، سَأَلَ، قَرَأَ
إ (on alif, below) start of word with kasra إِبْرَاهِيم، إِلَى، إِنَّ
ؤ (on و) middle/end near a damma سُؤَال، مُؤْمِن، لُؤْلُؤ
ئ (on ي-seat) middle/end near a kasra بِئْر، سَائِل، قَارِئ
ء (on the line) end after a long vowel or sukoon مَاء، سَمَاء، شَيْء، ضَوْء

For reading, she just needs to recognise all five as "hamza" and read the vowel on it. The spelling rule (which seat) comes much later.

Other small things she'll meet

  • لا : ل + ا is always written as one shape (لام ألف). On its own it means "no": لا. Inside words: سَلَام، أَوْلَاد، بِلَاد. Some fonts draw it as لا and some as لَا; both are the same two letters.
  • The silent alif after و in plural verbs: كَتَبُوا، ذَهَبُوا, read "katabuu". Just say: "this alif is silent."
  • Small alif (الألف الخنجرية) above a letter in some words: هَٰذَا، ذَٰلِكَ، لَٰكِنْ، الرَّحْمَٰن — read as a long "aa". Many children's books write هَذَا with the small alif; tell her it's a madd alif.
  • Two-letter words she'll see constantly: مِنْ، عَنْ، فِي، إِلَى، عَلَى، هَلْ، لَمْ، لَنْ، قَدْ، مَعَ، لا، مَا، يَا، أَنَا، أَنْتَ، أَنْتِ، هُوَ، هِيَ، هَذَا، هَذِهِ، مَنْ، مَاذَا، أَيْنَ، كَيْفَ، مَتَى.

Rule 8 — Reading sentences — Weeks 10–12

  • Stopping (الوَقْف): at the end of a sentence, drop the last vowel: ذَهَبَ الْوَلَدُ إِلَى الْمَدْرَسَةِ → read "…al-madrasah". Tanween at the stop: damm/kasr tanween become silent (قَلَمٌ → "qalam"), fath tanween becomes a long "aa" (قَلَمًا → "qalamaa").
  • Hamzat wasl in flow: ال and words like اِبْن، اِسْم lose their initial "a" when the previous word runs into them: فِي الْبَيْتِ = "fil-bayt".
  • Punctuation: Arabic comma is ،, question mark is ؟, full stop is . as in English.
  • Numbers: ٠ ١ ٢ ٣ ٤ ٥ ٦ ٧ ٨ ٩ — written left to right even inside Arabic text.

The order, one more time

  1. Joining (families + the six non-connectors) → 2. Madd → 3. Sukoon (+ ay/aw) → 4. Tanween → 5. Shadda → 6. ال sun/moon → 7. ة ى hamza → 8. Sentences.

This is essentially the order of the classical children's primers (نور البيان, القاعدة النورانية). If you have one of those books, this guide's weeks line up with its chapters.

How to Teach Arabic Reading and Writing to a 6–9-Year-Old

Read this once before Week 1. Come back to the last section when something isn't working.

The 25-minute day

Block Minutes What How it feels
1. Warm-up 3 Flash 10 letters in their joined shapes (ـبـ ـعـ ـهـ…); she says the letter. Then 5 letters with random vowels. A speed game
2. Reading page 10 This week's page from reading-pages.pdf. Every row three times (below). Reading with Mama
3. Writing 10 Trace (Weeks 1–2) → copy 4 words from the reading page → 3-word dictation from yesterday's page Quiet desk time
4. Story 5 You read her a vowelled Arabic story, finger under the words. She can join in on words she knows. Cuddle time

Five days a week. If a day goes badly, do only blocks 1 and 4 and call it done. If she is also doing school homework, put this before homework, not after: attention is a morning resource.

Rules that keep it working - One rule per week. The plan introduces exactly one new idea a week. Don't combine, don't rush. If Friday's reading is still slow, repeat the week with the same page. - Stop early. End the reading block while she's still winning. - She ticks the box. Every row on the page has a tick box. She ticks it herself after reading the row alone. Visible progress matters more at 7 than at 4. - Praise the mechanism, not the result: "You spotted the sukoon and glued it. That's exactly it." Not "good girl." - Mistakes are information. Don't say "no." Say "look again at the vowel on the ت," and wait.

Reading

How she reads a word (teach this in Week 1, keep it for months)

  1. Right edge first. Finger goes to the right end of the word.
  2. Tap and say: each letter with its vowel. كَ – تَ – بَ. (In Arabic classrooms this is called التَّهْجِئَة.)
  3. Slide and say: finger slides under the word, right to left, and she says it in one go: كَتَبَ.
  4. If it came out wrong, don't repeat the word for her. Go back to step 2 on the letter that went wrong.

When she's fluent on a row, she stops tapping and just slides. Tapping comes back automatically for any new hard word. That's the goal.

How to run a reading page (10 minutes)

Each page has 5–7 rows of words (later, sentences). Every row, three passes:

  1. You read, she follows. Your finger under the word, normal speed, exaggerated vowels. She watches and listens.
  2. Together. Both of you, her finger, slightly slower.
  3. Alone. She reads the row. Tick the box.

Then the bottom section: she copies 4 words from the page (writing block). Next day, the same page starts with a 1-minute reread of rows she ticked, then new rows. Most pages take 3–4 days to finish; the 5th day is a "read the whole page to Baba/Grandma" performance.

Say the sounds well

  • Short vowel: one beat. Long vowel: two beats. Clap it. Over-do it for the first month: "qa-a-la". Short/long confusion is the number one error, and it's an ear problem, not an eye problem.
  • Emphatic letters (ص ض ط ظ ق) are "heavy". Tell her: "make your mouth big and heavy." Pairs to contrast: سَ/صَ، تَ/طَ، دَ/ضَ، ذَ/ظَ، كَ/قَ. The "heavy" letters also darken the vowel: تِين vs طِين.
  • Throat pairs: هَ/حَ، أَ/عَ. Play "which one did I say?" with your hand on her hand so she feels it in your throat.
  • Sukoon = no vowel at all. Don't let "مِنْ" become "mi-nə". The ن is glued.

If Arabic is not the language spoken at home

Reading a word she doesn't know is fine: decoding is decoding. But meaning makes it stick, so: - After each row, ask "which word means…?" for 1–2 words. Every word on the printable pages is a real, common word. - Add 5 minutes of Arabic talk a day: name things at dinner, count, give simple commands (اِجْلِسِي، اِفْتَحِي الْبَاب). - Cartoons in fusha (e.g. the classic dubbed ones) with the vowelled subtitles off, just for the ear.

Writing

The three stages

  1. Forms (Weeks 1–2): tracing each letter in its 4 positions on ruled lines. writing-pages.pdf has one page per letter family.
  2. Joining (Weeks 1–4): "join the letters" (كَ + تَ + بَ → كَتَبَ) and the reverse, "split the word" (كَتَبَ → كَ ، تَ ، بَ). This is the same skill as reading, from the other side.
  3. Copying then dictation (from Week 2, every day): copy 4 words from the reading page; next day, 3 of those words from dictation (إِمْلَاء). Dictation is where reading rules turn into spelling rules.

The physical rules, in the order you'll need them

  • Right to left. Sticker on the right edge of the page = start. Move it only when she never needs it.
  • Sit on the line. Most letters sit on the baseline; some dip below (ج ح خ ع غ ص ض ق ل م ن ي ة at word end, and ر ز و always). The ruled writing paper in the printables has a baseline and a dip line.
  • Body first, dots after, vowels last. Write the whole word's bodies in one flow, then go back right-to-left adding dots, hamzas, and finally harakat. This is how adults write and it stops the "one dot missing" errors.
  • Dots are dots. One dot, two dots side by side, three in a triangle. Not dashes, not circles. Two dots may be written as a short dash in handwriting later, not now.
  • Pencil, ruled paper, big. Letters about 1 cm tall for the first month. Two-line paper (two lines with a gap) makes ascender letters (ا ل ك ط ظ) easy to size.
  • Tooth family in the middle is the hardest: ـبـ ـتـ ـثـ ـنـ ـيـ all look the same body; only dots differ. Practise words like بِنْت، تِين، نَبِيّ، ثَبَتَ where teeth are next to each other, and count the teeth: "one, two, three teeth, dots on the second."
  • The six non-connectors: after ا د ذ ر ز و, lift the pencil and start the next letter fresh. Say "let go" out loud in Week 1.

How to teach a letter family (one writing session)

  1. Show the family's page. Point out what stays (the body) and what changes (the tail).
  2. She traces each form once with her finger, saying its position: "at the start, in the middle, at the end."
  3. Trace the row in pencil.
  4. Write the example words (they are on the page), copying from the model.
  5. Done. One family per session; some families take two sessions (ط ظ ع غ and ه).

Dictation, the gentle way

  • 3 words, from yesterday's reading page, always ones she has copied already.
  • Say the word slowly, twice, with clear vowels. She writes. Don't spell it for her.
  • Then she checks her own word against the page and fixes it herself with a different colour. Finding your own mistake is the learning.
  • Keep a "Words I can spell" list (in 05-progress-tracker.md).

Things that look like problems but aren't

What you see What it means What to do
She reads the first letter and guesses the word She's treating it like memorised sight words Thumb over the rest of the word, reveal one letter at a time
Reads كَتَبَ as "kataba" one day, "kutub" the next Ignoring vowels Back to tap-and-say for a week; she must say each vowel
Says "ka-a-taba" for كَتَبَ (adds length) Short/long ear confusion Clap game; minimal pairs (جَمَل/جَمَال)
Can't find ـعـ or ـهـ in the middle of a word Those two middle forms don't look like the letter Extra time on family 5 and 7 pages; "find the ع" hunt in a story

Forgets dots, or puts them on the wrong tooth Writing dots too early, mid-word Body first, dots after. Count the teeth.
Writes ه for ة or ي for ى at the end Doesn't yet know the ending rules Wait for Week 9; until then just correct the dot without comment
Leaves the alif off tanween fath (كِتَابً) Very common; the alif looks silent so she skips it "Tanween fath rides an alif." Check it in every dictation.
Reads sun-letter words with the ل ("al-shams") Hasn't automated the ل rule Highlighter game: she highlights the ل she should NOT say before reading the row
Reads fluently but slowly That's just fluency arriving Reread. Same page 3 days = speed. That's not cheating.
Resists the whole thing Sessions too long, or too many rules at once Cut to 10 minutes, one page, for a week; games from the activity bank only

The 12-Week Plan

One rule a week. Each week has one reading page (printables/reading-pages.pdf, page number = week number) and its writing work. Repeat any week that isn't fluent by Friday; the plan is a sequence, not a calendar.

Daily: warm-up 3 min → reading page 10 min → writing 10 min → story 5 min. Weekends off.

Codes: RP = reading page, WP = writing page (printables/writing-pages.pdf), AB # = activity in 04-activity-bank.md.

What comes after Week 12

  • Reading: graded vowelled readers, 10 minutes a day; then Qur'an-style reading practice if that's a goal (the rules above are exactly the ones a Qaida course assumes); gradually text with partial tashkeel (Grade 3–4 school books).
  • Writing: her own sentences daily (a 3-line diary in Arabic), spelling rules for hamza seats, the difference between ة/ت endings, and cursive speed.
  • Grammar is not for now. Reading fluency and a big word bank first.

If a week goes badly

  • Resisting: cut to 10 minutes, one page, games only, for a week. It's usually "too long," not "too hard."
  • A rule isn't sticking: repeat the week. Or go back one rule; a shaky sukoon is often a shaky madd underneath.
  • She's flying: don't add rules faster. Add reading: more books, more rows, longer stories. Fluency needs miles, not new rules.

Activity Bank — 35 Short Games

Each takes 3–8 minutes. Swap one in whenever the page feels flat. Numbers match the plan's "Game of the week."

Joining letters and letter forms (Weeks 1–2)

  1. Magnet word-building. Arabic magnetic letters (or letters written on bottle caps) on a tray. You say كَتَبَ, she builds it right-to-left, then reads it. Then swap one letter: كَتَبَ → كَتَمَ → رَسَمَ.
  2. Find the body. A page of a vowelled story. "Find every ب, in any position." Highlighter. Count them.
  3. Let go! Cards for ا د ذ ر ز و in red, all other letters in blue. She builds words; at each red card she claps once ("let go") and starts the next letter apart.
  4. Body-and-tail sort. Cards showing letters in middle form (ـبـ ـعـ ـهـ ـكـ …). She sorts them into the 7 family piles by body.
  5. Letter dominoes. Cards with a letter's final form on one side and another's initial form on the other; match to make joined pairs.
  6. Sand tray forms. Write a letter's 4 forms in a tray of semolina with a finger, saying "alone, start, middle, end."
  7. Which one changed? Two words that differ by one letter (بَابَ/نَابَ, سَمِعَ/سَمِحَ). She spots and reads both.

Long vowels (Weeks 3–4)

  1. Clap the vowel. You say a word; she claps once for a short vowel, twice for a long one. Then reverse.
  2. Long-or-short cards. Pairs: جَمَل/جَمَال، عَلَم/عَالَم، كَتَبَ/كَاتَبَ، مَلِك/مَالِك. Shuffle, draw, read, say which.
  3. Stretch the elastic. Hold a rubber band; stretch it while saying the long vowel. Physical = memorable.
  4. Madd hunt. In a story page, circle every alif/waw/yaa that is a madd (no vowel on it). Trick: some و and ي are consonants.

Sukoon (Week 5)

  1. Glue-it robot. You say a word letter by letter; at the sukoon she says "glue!" and joins it to the previous letter. مِـ – نْ → "min".
  2. Sukoon cards. Word cards with the sukoon covered by a sticky flag. She reads, lifts the flag, reads again correctly.
  3. ay/aw pairs. بَيْت/بَات، يَوْم/يُوم(nonsense), عَيْن/عِين: which is real, which is silly? Read both.

Tanween (Week 6)

  1. Three-cup tanween. Three cups labelled ـٌ ـً ـٍ. Say a word ("kitaabun", "kitaaban", "kitaabin"); she drops a bead in the right cup, then writes the word with the right ending.
  2. Alif police. Dictate tanween-fath words; she must "arrest" any missing alif with a red circle.
  3. Rhyme the ending. She names three things in the room with tanween damm: بَابٌ، قَلَمٌ، كُرْسِيٌّ.

Shadda (Week 7)

  1. Clap-clap shadda. Read a word; on the shadda letter, clap twice. سَيَّارَة = sa-y-ya-ra.
  2. Double or not. Pairs: دَرَسَ/دَرَّسَ، عَلِمَ/عَلَّمَ، كَبَرَ/كَبَّرَ. Say one; she points and reads.
  3. Shadda relay. Write a letter twice on the board (بْبَ), then she rewrites it as بَّ. 10 letters, timed.

ال sun and moon (Week 8)

  1. Sun & moon sort. Word cards with ال; a sun and a moon drawn on two plates. Sort, then read each pile aloud.
  2. Highlight the silent ل. Story page: she highlights every ل in ال that must not be said (sun letters), then reads.
  3. The moon sentence. Learn إِبْغِ حَجَّكَ وَخَفْ عَقِيمَهُ as a chant; every letter in it is a moon letter.

Endings and hamza (Week 9)

  1. Ending detective. Cards ending ة / ه / ى / ي. She says the sound at a stop and when continuing.
  2. Hamza hunt. In a page of text, circle all five hamza shapes in five colours.
  3. Girl or boy. Nouns with and without ة: مُعَلِّم/مُعَلِّمَة، طَبِيب/طَبِيبَة. Read, sort, giggle.

Sentences and stories (Weeks 10–12)

  1. Sentence strips. Cut a sentence into words; she rebuilds it right-to-left and reads.
  2. Stop sign. A stop card at the end of each sentence: she drops the last vowel there.
  3. Who did what? After reading a sentence: مَنْ؟ مَاذَا فَعَلَ؟ أَيْنَ؟ Three questions, answered in Arabic.
  4. Story theatre. She reads the story; you act it with toys. Then swap.
  5. Label the house. Sticky notes: الْبَاب، الْمَطْبَخ، السَّرِير، الشُّبَّاك. She writes them with full tashkeel.
  6. Diary line. Every evening one sentence: الْيَوْمَ أَكَلْتُ … / لَعِبْتُ … She writes; you don't correct spelling in the diary, only in dictation.

Any week

  1. Speed row. Time her reading a row she's already ticked. Beat yesterday's time. Chart it.
  2. Teach the little sister. She teaches Ayah one letter or one word. Nothing cements like teaching.
  3. Certificate day. End of month: a certificate, the "Words I can read" list read aloud to the family, and she picks the next story book.

Progress Tracker

Tick as she masters each item. "Mastered" = she does it without prompting, three days in a row.

Reading rules

  • ☐ Reads any letter with fatha / kasra / damma instantly (Rule 0)
  • ☐ Recognises the 7 letter families in all positions
  • ☐ Knows the 6 non-connectors (ا د ذ ر ز و) and reads words that "let go" correctly
  • ☐ Reads 3-letter short-vowel words without tapping (كَتَبَ)
  • ☐ Reads long vowels ا و ي with two beats (قَالَ، يَقُولُ، فِي)
  • ☐ Hears the difference جَمَل / جَمَال
  • ☐ Reads sukoon correctly, glued (مِنْ، يَكْتُبُ)
  • ☐ Reads ay / aw (بَيْت، يَوْم)
  • ☐ Reads all three tanween (كِتَابٌ ـًا ـٍ)
  • ☐ Reads shadda as a doubled letter (مُعَلِّم)
  • ☐ Reads ال with moon letters (الْقَمَر)
  • ☐ Reads ال with sun letters, silent ل (الشَّمْس)
  • ☐ Reads ة correctly at a stop and in flow
  • ☐ Reads ى as long "aa" (عَلَى)
  • ☐ Recognises all 5 hamza shapes
  • ☐ Drops the final vowel at the end of a sentence
  • ☐ Reads a 5-line vowelled story with fewer than 3 stumbles
  • ☐ Reads a 10-line vowelled story aloud to someone else

Writing

  • ☐ Writes right to left without the sticker
  • ☐ Writes all 7 families in 4 positions on ruled lines
  • ☐ Lifts the pencil after the 6 non-connectors
  • ☐ Writes bodies first, then dots, then vowels
  • ☐ Copies 4 words a day with all vowels
  • ☐ Takes a 3-word dictation with at most 1 error
  • ☐ Writes tanween fath with its alif
  • ☐ Writes shadda words correctly (no doubled letter written)
  • ☐ Writes ال words (including silent-ل sun words with the ل still written)
  • ☐ Chooses ة vs ه and ى vs ي at word ends
  • ☐ Writes a dictated sentence
  • ☐ Writes 3 sentences of her own

If she's stuck on…

  • Joining (Week 1–2): Do only family pages and magnet-building for a week. Reading joined words is 90% "spot the body." Play "Find the body" (AB #2) on real books.
  • Long vowels: it's the ear. Clap games with no text for three days, then back to the page.
  • Sukoon: check madd first; if she's stretching everything, sukoon can't land. Then "glue-it robot" daily.
  • Tanween fath alif: dictation with "alif police" for a week.
  • Sun letters: highlighter game. Then chant the moon sentence; everything else is sun.
  • Writing direction: don't fight it in the reading block; fix it only in the writing block, calmly, every time. It resolves by itself around 8.
  • Speed: rereading. Three days on the same page always works. A page she can read fast is worth more than two she can read slowly.
  • Motivation: shorter sessions, a visible chart, and teaching Ayah. Never skip the story at the end; that is the part she'll remember loving.
