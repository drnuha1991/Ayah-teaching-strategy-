# Resources and Recommendations

Everything below is judged against three things: **a 4-year-old who gets bored easily**,
**learns best through play**, and **a parent with one hour a day**. Prices are approximate US
prices as of 2026; check current pricing before buying.

## Bottom line first

| Skill | Buy this | Cost | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Math** | *Preschool Math at Home* by Kate Snow | ~$20 | Made exactly for age 3–5. Five-minute games, no workbook, uses things you own. Reviewers consistently say kids think it's play. |
| **Handwriting / pre-writing** | *Handwriting Without Tears: Get Set for School* (Pre-K workbook) | ~$15–20 | "Crayon only", multisensory, teaches strokes then capitals in a smart order. Built for active 4-year-olds. |
| **First books to read** | *Bob Books Set 1: Beginning Readers* | ~$15 | 12 tiny books that use only the sounds she'll learn in Month 2. Finishing a whole book is a huge confidence win. |
| **Phonics spine** | *ABC See, Hear, Do* Level 1 (Stefanie Hohl) | ~$15–20 | Letters in groups of 4–5 with a picture + hand motion, then CVC words from just those letters. The plan in this repo follows it. |
| **Optional extra spine** | *The Good and the Beautiful Kindergarten Prep* **or** *Logic of English Foundations A* | Free PDF / ~$100 | Only if you want more lesson scripting. See comparison below. |
| **Free app** | Khan Academy Kids | Free | No ads, no purchases, covers reading and math, genuinely well designed. |
| **Tools** | Magnetic letters (lowercase!), cookie sheet, playdough, chalk, tweezers/tongs, dice, pom-poms, index cards | ~$20–30 | Used in almost every activity in this guide. |

**Budget path (~$70):** Preschool Math at Home + HWT Get Set for School + Bob Books Set 1 + magnetic letters + TGTB Kindergarten Prep free PDF + Khan Kids.

**"I want it all open-and-go" path (~$170):** the same, but Logic of English Foundations A instead of the TGTB PDF.

## Phonics and blending

Ayah already knows letter names, so the job is: **letter names → letter sounds → hearing sounds blend
(orally) → blending printed 3-letter words → reading little books.** Any program you choose should do
those steps in that order. All of these do.

### ABC See, Hear, Do — Level 1 (top pick, and what this plan follows)

- **What it is:** A ~$15–20 picture book (26 lessons, one per letter) that teaches each capital letter's sound with an animal picture and a hand motion. Letters come **in groups of 4–5, not A to Z**, and after every group the child sounds out CVC words using only those letters (the first group gets *cat* and *mat*). By the end she has read 55 words, and a review page adds 48 more. Level 2 does the same with lowercase; later levels add blends, digraphs, and sight words. There's also a matching activity guide, coloring book, and writing-practice book per level.
- **Pros:** Cheap. Multisensory (see, hear, do) so it suits a wiggly 4-year-old. Blending starts in the first week, which keeps her motivated. Simple enough that you don't need a teacher's manual.
- **Cons:** It's a book, not a full program: no games, no writing, no math. That's what this repo adds around it. Do one letter a day and the group's words all week; don't rush through the book in a fortnight.
- **Verdict:** Use it as the phonics spine. Buy Level 2 (lowercase) around Week 10.

### The Good and the Beautiful (TGTB) — Preschool and Kindergarten Prep

- **What it is:** A gentle, open-and-go homeschool curriculum. The *Preschool* course (ages 3–5) covers letter names and sounds, early math, and fine motor skills. The *Kindergarten Prep* course book (30 lessons) is the bridge to kindergarten: letter sounds, blending, and beginning handwriting. Cathy Duffy's review notes Kindergarten Prep takes "a few months rather than a year", which matches this 3-month plan almost exactly.
- **Pros:** Very affordable (the course PDFs are free to download from their site; printed books are around $30–40). Beautiful, calm design. Short lessons. Parents describe it as "not just cute, it's solid, structured, and it works".
- **Cons you should know:**
  - It is a **faith-based (Christian) curriculum.** Stories, songs, and examples include religious content. Some families love that; if it doesn't fit your family, choose Logic of English instead.
  - Kindergarten Prep asks for more pencil control than many 4-year-olds have (Cathy Duffy's review makes the same point). Skip or shrink the writing parts and do handwriting through HWT instead.
  - The Preschool course will feel too easy on letter *names* for Ayah, since she already knows them. If you go TGTB, start with **Kindergarten Prep** and go slowly, or just use the Preschool course's letter-*sound* pages.
- **Verdict:** Great choice if the faith content is welcome and you want cheap and simple. Use it as the spine and add the games from this repo.

### Logic of English — Foundations A

- **What it is:** A secular, research-based (Orton-Gillingham style) program for ages 4–7. Level A teaches letter sounds, phonemic awareness, blending, and handwriting all together, through lots of games and movement.
- **Pros:** Genuinely play-based; reviewers say it's "very motivating" for even 3-year-olds. Teaches *all* the sounds a letter can make from the start (e.g., that "a" says three sounds), which pays off later. Includes handwriting, so you can skip a separate writing program. 90-day money-back guarantee.
- **Cons:** Most expensive option (~$95–100 for teacher's manual + workbook, plus $40–60 in manipulatives like phonogram cards and game pieces). Lessons are written for 30–60 minutes; **you must split them across days** to stay under an hour and keep her attention. It is slower-paced than it needs to be for a child who already knows letters.
- **Verdict:** Best all-in-one if you want one purchase that covers reading and writing and you don't want religious content. Do half a lesson a day.

### Teach Your Child to Read in 100 Easy Lessons

- **What it is:** A $15–20 scripted book, 20 minutes a day, based on Direct Instruction. Very strong research track record.
- **Cons:** Dry. Uses a special font with marks that she'll later have to unlearn. The reviews and parent reports agree: it works, but children (especially 3–4-year-olds) often **tire of it once the stories get longer**. For a child who gets bored easily, this is the risky pick.
- **Verdict:** Only if she likes structure and you like a script. Not my first choice for Ayah.

### All About Reading — Pre-reading

- **What it is:** ~$90 kit with a puppet (Ziggy), letter cards, and activity sheets covering letter names, sounds, and rhyming. Very fun, very gentle.
- **Cons:** It does **not teach blending**; that's Level 1 (another ~$120). Since Ayah already knows letter names, half of Pre-reading is review. Poor value here.
- **Verdict:** Skip. You'd outgrow it in a month.

### Bob Books (Set 1: Beginning Readers)

- Twelve small books, each using only a few letters, starting with "Mat sat." Perfect first readers for Month 2–3 of the plan. Set 2 continues the same way. Around $15 per set. **Buy Set 1.**

### Free reading resources worth using

- **Khan Academy Kids** (app, free, no ads): letter sounds, blending, and math, wrapped in games. Best free option by far. Use it as a 10-minute "screen block" when you need a break, not as the main lesson.
- **Teach Your Monster to Read** (free on a web browser; the app costs a few dollars): a game where a monster learns sounds and blends. Kids love it. Matches Month 1–2 of this plan.
- **Starfall ABCs** (free part of Starfall): letter sounds with animations.
- **Progressive Phonics** (free printable PDFs): little stories where you read most of the words and she reads the highlighted ones. Nice for Month 3.
- **Your library**: ask for "decodable readers" or "phonics readers." Many libraries have Bob Books to borrow.

## Math

### Preschool Math at Home — Kate Snow (top pick)

- **What it is:** A ~$20 book of simple, sequenced games for ages 3–5: counting, comparing, subitizing (seeing "three" without counting), shapes, patterns, and the first ideas of adding and taking away. Uses household items only. Each activity is about 5 minutes.
- **Why it fits Ayah:** It's literally "math as playing." Reviewers report their 4-year-olds called each activity a "math game." No worksheets, no writing required, so it doesn't fight with her fine-motor stage. It also matches her level exactly: she counts to 10, and this builds real number *understanding* on top of that.
- **What comes next:** When she turns 5, move to *Kindergarten Math with Confidence* by the same author (~$40 for instructor guide + workbook). Families report that transition is smooth.

### Kindergarten Math with Confidence

- Excellent, hands-on, 15–20 minute lessons with a review warm-up, an activity, then a short workbook page. **Save this for age 5.** Starting it at 4 usually means the workbook pages become a fight.

### The Good and the Beautiful Math K

- Fine if you're already all-in on TGTB and want one look and feel. Otherwise Preschool Math at Home is a better fit for this age and for one-hour days.

### Free math resources

- **Khan Academy Kids** (again).
- **Board games** teach more math than worksheets at this age: *Count Your Chickens*, *Hi Ho! Cherry-O*, *Hoot Owl Hoot*, *Candy Land* (colors, turn-taking), and later *Sum Swamp*. Dominoes and dice are gold for subitizing.
- **YouTube:** *Numberblocks* (BBC) is the best math show for this age. 5-minute episodes. It's how many 4-year-olds learn number bonds without noticing.

## Writing and drawing

### Handwriting Without Tears — Get Set for School (Pre-K)

- **What it is:** The preschool level of the most widely used handwriting program in US schools. "Crayon only." Teaches pre-writing strokes, then **capital letters** in a developmental order (starting with the easy straight-line ones: L, F, E, H, T, I), plus numbers. Optional extras: wooden pieces to build letters, the "Mat Man" song, a slate chalkboard for "wet-dry-try."
- **Pros:** Multisensory, songs, building letters with pieces before writing them. One reviewer said it was a great fit for a *very active* 4-year-old because it "held their attention" with lots of ways to practice the same letter. Rated around 4/5 by parents.
- **Cons:** The workbook alone is a little dull if you skip the hands-on parts. You don't need the full kit; the **workbook (~$15–20) plus homemade wood pieces or playdough** is enough.
- **Verdict:** Buy the workbook. Do the activities in `04-activity-bank.md` before each page.

### Drawing

You do **not** need a drawing curriculum at 4. Drawing *is* handwriting prep: every circle, line and zigzag she draws builds the hand for letters. What helps:

- **Free drawing daily** with chunky crayons, markers, and chalk. Big paper, and vertical surfaces (easel, paper taped to a wall, sidewalk chalk) build shoulder and wrist strength.
- **Art for Kids Hub** (YouTube, free): "how to draw" videos with a dad and his kids. Pick the simplest ones.
- **Ed Emberley's *Drawing Book of Animals*** (~$10): shapes-to-animals drawing. She can follow the little steps with you.
- **Process art:** painting with q-tips, sponge stamping, cutting with kid scissors, sticker collages. All of these are fine-motor practice.

## What to NOT buy yet

- Any **lowercase cursive** or extensive **lowercase** handwriting program. Capitals first at 4.
- **Sight-word flashcard packs.** She'll read most "sight words" by sounding out anyway. A handful (the, a, I, is, to, and) is enough for now.
- **Full kindergarten box curricula.** She's 4. Three light subjects and a lot of play is the right dose.
- **Tablet apps beyond Khan Kids and Teach Your Monster.** Most "learning" apps are candy with a letter on it.
