# Teaching Ayah at Home — Pre-K Guide (Age 4)

A practical, play-based plan for teaching a 4-year-old who already knows the alphabet
and can count to 10 how to **blend letters and read, write, and do early math**, in
**no more than 1 hour a day**, split into short bursts so she doesn't get bored.

## The short version

1. **Buy three things** (about $55 total): *Preschool Math at Home* (Kate Snow), *Handwriting Without Tears: Get Set for School* workbook, and *Bob Books Set 1*. Get a set of **magnetic letters** and a **cookie sheet** too.
2. **For phonics**, pick one spine: *The Good and the Beautiful Kindergarten Prep* (free PDF, gentle, but contains Christian content) or *Logic of English Foundations A* (secular, more games, about $100). Or follow the plan in this repo with free resources; it's designed to work on its own.
3. **Install Khan Academy Kids** (free, no ads). It's the best "screen time that's actually learning" for this age.
4. **Daily rhythm:** four 10–15 minute blocks with movement in between. Reading, writing, math, then play/read-aloud. Stop each block *before* she's done, not after.
5. **Follow the 12-week plan.** Month 1 = letter sounds + oral blending. Month 2 = blending real words. Month 3 = reading little books and sentences.

## Golden rules for a 4-year-old

- **Sounds, not names.** She knows "bee, see, dee". Reading needs /b/ /k/ /d/. Month 1 is about moving from names to sounds.
- **Short beats long.** 10 focused minutes beats 30 distracted ones. If she's wiggling, it's over.
- **Play is the lesson, not the reward.** Every skill in this plan has a game version. Use the game version first.
- **Hands before pencil.** Playdough, tweezers, chalk and finger-tracing come before worksheets. Pencil grip and letter formation come from strong hands.
- **Read to her every day.** 15–20 minutes of you reading picture books is the single most powerful thing in this whole plan. It counts as part of the hour.
- **Never test, only play.** If she gets it wrong, just say the right answer cheerfully and move on. No drilling.
- **Take days off.** 4–5 days a week is plenty. Progress at this age comes in bursts, not straight lines.
