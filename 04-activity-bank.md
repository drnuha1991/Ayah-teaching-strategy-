# Activity Bank

Short, cheap, play-based activities organized by skill. Numbers are referenced from the weekly plan.
Most take 5–10 minutes and use things you already have. **Swap freely.** If she's bored with one, pick another for the same skill.

**Supply basket:** lowercase magnetic letters, cookie sheet, index cards, playdough, chalk, salt/sand in a tray, tweezers or tongs, pom-poms, dice, dominoes, sticky notes, a muffin tin, stickers, crayons (broken short), kid scissors, a puppet or stuffed animal.

---

## A. Letter sounds (Month 1)

1. **Sound flash race** — flash cards of learned letters; she says the sound. Time it with a silly countdown; try to beat yesterday. 2 minutes.
2. **Trick the puppet** — the puppet says the wrong sound ("t says /m/!"). She corrects it, giggling. Kids learn most from catching mistakes.
3. **Sound treasure hunt** — hide 5 objects starting with today's sound (sock, spoon, sticker, straw, stone for /s/). She hunts, names each, says the sound.
4. **Letter-sound hopscotch** — chalk letters in hopscotch squares. She jumps and says each sound. Add "jump to the letter that says /m/."
5. **Sensory writing** — write the letter in a salt tray, shaving cream, or finger paint while saying the sound. Then "erase" and do it again.
6. **Robot dinner** — one meal where every request is in robot talk: "pass the c-u-p." Builds oral blending without any letters.
7. **Sound sorting bins** — two bins, two letters. She sorts toys by first sound. (Pig, pen, pot → /p/; sock, spoon → /s/.)
8. **I Spy with sounds** — "I spy something that starts with /b/." Then she gives you one.
9. **Sound actions** — each letter gets an action (look up "Jolly Phonics actions" on YouTube). Play "Simon says /s/."
10. **Alphabet bath** — foam letters in the bath; fish out the one that says /t/. Stick it to the wall and say a word.
11. **Rhyme time** — "cat, hat, bat, ___?" Then silly nonsense rhymes: "zat, wat!" Rhyming trains the ear for blending.

## B. Blending printed words (Month 2–3)

12. **Word family flip book** — staple 6 index cards on the left of a card that reads *_at*; flip the first letter: *c, h, m, b, s, r*. She reads each flip.
13. **Blending ramp** — three letter cards lined up next to a cardboard ramp. Roll a toy car past them as she blends; roll faster each time until the word "pops out."
14. **Cookie-sheet swap** — magnets: *sat*. "Change one letter to make *mat*." She swaps. Then *map, mop, top, tip*. One swap per turn.
15. **Secret word treasure hunt** — 5 index cards with CVC words that are also hiding places (*bed, mat, cup, tub, box*). Each word she reads leads to the next clue; the last hides a sticker.
16. **Word building with blocks** — write letters on blocks/Duplos; she stacks them to build the word you say ("build *sun*").
17. **Sentence strips** — silly sentences on paper strips: *the pig can hop. the cat is wet.* She reads and acts it out.
18. **Read to Teddy** — she reads each word she built to a stuffed animal, who "claps."
19. **Reading picnic** — blanket, snacks, a pile of Bob Books; every stuffed animal gets read one page.
20. **Whisper-shout reading** — read the same 5 words in a whisper, then a shout, then a squeaky voice. Rereading = fluency.
21. **Label the house** — sticky notes with CVC words on the real objects (*bed, cup, pan, mat, box, tub*). She reads them as she passes all week.
22. **Story cards about her** — write a 2-line story on an index card: *Ayah can run. Ayah is 4.* She reads it and keeps it in her "my books" envelope.
23. **Word swat** — tape 6 words on the wall. Say one; she swats it with a fly swatter and reads it.
24. **Magic reveal** — write CVC words in white crayon; she paints over them with watercolor to reveal, then reads.
25. **Progressive Phonics books** (free printables) — you read the black words, she reads the red ones.

## C. Hands and handwriting

26. **Playdough letters** — roll snakes, build today's capital. Then squish and rebuild. Hand strength + letter shape.
27. **Popsicle-stick letters** — big sticks (long line) and half sticks (little line) build L, F, E, H, T, I like HWT's wood pieces. Curves: a bent pipe cleaner.
28. **Wet-Dry-Try** — small chalkboard. You write the letter in chalk; she traces with a wet sponge, then a dry paper towel, then writes it in chalk herself.
29. **Salt tray strokes** — pre-writing strokes (|, —, O, +, X, □) in a tray of salt. Say the stroke: "big line down."
30. **Rainbow letters** — one big letter on paper; she traces it with 5 different crayon colors.
31. **Vertical writing** — paper taped to the wall, easel, or bath crayons on the tub wall. Builds the wrist and shoulder for good grip.
32. **Tweezers transfer** — move pom-poms from a bowl into a muffin tin with tweezers or tongs. Count them as a bonus.
33. **Cutting strips** — kid scissors, cut a paper strip into "fringe," then cut along a straight line, then a zigzag.
34. **Sticker lines** — put dot stickers along a line, then along a circle, then along a letter's path.
35. **Squeeze and squirt** — spray bottle at chalk letters outside; eyedropper into an ice tray; both build finger strength.
36. **Name puzzle** — her name on a card, cut into letters; she rebuilds it, then traces it, then writes it.
37. **Draw-and-label** — she draws a cat; you write "CAT" lightly; she traces it. Labeling drawings makes writing feel purposeful.
38. **Lacing** — cardboard shape with holes and a shoelace.
39. **Air writing** — whole arm, huge letters in the air, eyes closed, saying the strokes. Great for wiggly days.
40. **Certificate day** — she "signs" her name on a homemade certificate at the end of the 12 weeks.

## D. Math

### Counting and number sense
41. **Touch and count** — line up snacks; she touches each one as she counts. "So how many?" (cardinality). Eat them (subtraction!).
42. **Counting collections** — a jar of buttons/rocks/LEGO. Count how many, then "make it 10."
43. **Count the steps** — stairs, steps to the mailbox, jumps on the trampoline. Rote counting to 20–30 in real life.
44. **Countdown rocket** — 10 fingers, count down to blast-off while crouching then jumping.
45. **Number hunt** — find numerals around the house (clock, remote, book pages, mailbox). "Which number is this?"
46. **Muffin tin numbers** — number cards 1–10 in muffin cups; she puts that many pom-poms in each (tweezers for bonus fine motor).
47. **Hundred chart pointing** — a printed chart; point and count to 20, then 30. Cover a number; "which one is hiding?"

### Subitizing (seeing how many without counting)
48. **Dice grab** — roll a die, grab that many blocks. "Don't count, just look!" Works for 1–4, then 5–6.
49. **Dot card flash** — homemade cards with 1–5 dots in different arrangements; flash for 1 second. "How many?"
50. **Domino match** — turn dominoes face up, find two sides with the same number of dots.
51. **Finger flash** — you flash fingers behind your back and show them for one second; she says the number. Then she does it to you.
52. **Five-frame** — draw 5 boxes; put pom-poms in some. "How many? How many empty spaces?" (number bonds of 5).

### Comparing, adding, taking away
53. **More or fewer?** — two piles of grapes; "which has more? How do you know? Let's line them up to check."
54. **Hide the pom-poms** — 5 pom-poms; cover some with a cup. "How many are hiding?" Number bonds without any symbols.
55. **One more, one less** — she has 3 crackers. "I'll give you one more. Now how many?" "The dog ate one. Now?"
56. **Snack math** — "You have 2 apple slices and I have 3. How many altogether? Line them up."
57. **Roll and add** — two dice, take that many blocks each roll, count the tower.
58. **Toy story problems** — "3 cars are in the garage. 2 drive away. How many are left?" with real toy cars.

### Shapes, patterns, measurement
59. **Shape hunt** — find 3 circles, 3 rectangles in the house. Photograph them.
60. **Popsicle-stick shapes** — build a triangle (3 sticks), a square (4). "How many sides?"
61. **Pattern beads** — string beads red-blue-red-blue; then red-red-blue. She continues your pattern, then makes one for you.
62. **Body patterns** — clap-stomp-clap-stomp; she continues. Then invents one.
63. **Longer or shorter?** — line up spoons, forks, crayons. Order 3 things from shortest to longest.
64. **Kitchen scale / cup measuring** — pour water into cups: "which holds more?" Bath time counts.

### Games that teach math without trying
65. **Board games** — *Count Your Chickens* (cooperative, counting and spinner), *Hi Ho! Cherry-O* (counting, taking away), *Hoot Owl Hoot* (colors, cooperative), *Candy Land* (colors, turn-taking). Play one every day of "board game week" (Week 11).
66. **Numberblocks** — 5-minute episodes on YouTube; watch one, then build that number with blocks.
67. **Khan Academy Kids** — 10 minutes of the math path when you need a break.
